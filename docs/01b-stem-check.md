# SaltDrag STEM check

Verdict: the working principle (two people wind a geared drum on dry ground and the rope draws a rake across the pan) sits within known science, and so do the principles of options B and C. The first check found four details that failed on the TRL 3 design of 2026-10-03: each cranker worked at 84 W against a sustained 75 W (S15), the 4.5 mm drum barrel kept a margin of only about 1 under three layers of rope at twice the rated pull (S7), three bolted joints could not be assembled as drawn (S26), and the assumption that several families share one set did not survive a throughput check (S24). The independent review then refuted five more verdicts: the brake would not hold with a wet lining (S9), one chain can take most of the pull (S11), the 25 mm crank shaft was short of its margin once C45's yield is taken for its size and of fatigue life in salt air (S5, S17), the gear and ratchet keys were overloaded (S22), and the rake would pitch onto its blade with the bridles at mid-height (S29). Each was fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 and is recorded in SDG-DEC-001 (D17 to D24); the rows below are on the fixed design. Of the 31 rows for option A, **25 are within known science and 6 are plausible, to confirm at TRL 4 with field data, a test or a datasheet**: the drag per metre of rake (S1, on which S2, S15, S23 and S30 depend), the drum barrel's multi-layer rope pressure (S7, reopened by the review), the anchor holding in the soil behind the bund (S12), safe working hours in the harvest heat (S16), 316 stainless in brine (S19) and the rope's life over the roller and on the bed (S27). None can be closed by calculation, so `stem_check` is **open**. Method and rules: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which reads the results of SDG-CAL-001.
